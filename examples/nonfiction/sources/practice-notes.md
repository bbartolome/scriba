# Notes on deliberate practice

Deliberate practice is effortful work aimed at a specific weakness. It is not the same as playing the piece again from the top.

Three features separate it from ordinary repetition:

- The goal is narrow. A pianist works on one passage, not the whole concerto.
- The attempt is slightly beyond current ability, so errors are common.
- Feedback arrives quickly, often within seconds, so the next attempt can change.

Most adults spend the bulk of their working time in activities that feel productive but lack one of these features. That is not a failing of character. The activities that give feedback are often harder to arrange.
