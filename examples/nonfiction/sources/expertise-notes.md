# Notes on expertise and plateaus

A plateau is a point where practice continues but improvement stops. It usually means the routine has become automatic. The performer can repeat the task well enough that the nervous system no longer needs to adapt.

The way out is to change the task in a small, measurable way: a faster tempo, a harder opening, a new constraint. The change should be just large enough to produce errors, and the errors should be easy to see.

Experts also tend to keep a written record of their mistakes. The record is not a diary. It is a list of the specific moves that failed, with a note on what to try next time.
